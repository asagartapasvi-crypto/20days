Syllabus-to-Source Mapping

Paper: 304 – Advertising and Market Research

Syllabus: Campaign planning – stages of campaign planning – types of approach – consumer behaviour – principles of campaign planning – allocation of advertising budget.

Mapping approach: The syllabus is treated as the controlling source. Each point is mapped only to material actually identified in the supplied sources. Direct, related/partial and gap coverage are clearly distinguished.

1. Campaign Planning

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
Advertising strategy is presented as part of the advertising management process. The source says advertising strategy should support the marketing strategy and company plans, and that strategic planning should be creative and flexible. It also explains setting objectives, formulating budget, creating advertising messages and selecting media/vehicles.

Direct and strong coverage. Main source for Unit 4. Study campaign planning as part of the wider advertising-management process.

2. Stages of Campaign Planning

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
The source gives a clear sequence under advertising strategy: setting objectives → formulating budget → creating advertising messages → selecting media and vehicles. Strategy implementation follows, including assigning roles/responsibilities, deadlines and a communication plan. Advertising effectiveness is then assessed.

Direct and strong coverage. This is the closest supplied-source mapping to the syllabus phrase 'stages of campaign planning'.

3. Types of Approach

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
The source identifies push and pull techniques as strategic choices depending on business goals. It also contains advertising techniques such as Above the Line, Below the Line and Through the Line, and different advertising appeals.

Related/strong coverage, but the exact syllabus wording 'types of approach' is not defined as a separate heading. Do not assume a particular classification beyond what the supplied source actually gives.

4. Consumer Behaviour

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
The advertising source repeatedly links campaign planning with customers and target audiences. Objectives may focus on customer purchase/action or long-term change in brand attitude. Media planning requires understanding customer media consumption, audience profile and campaign goals.

Strong related coverage. The source supports the consumer/target-audience side of planning, but a standalone detailed consumer-behaviour chapter was not identified.

5. Principles of Campaign Planning

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
The media-mix section gives practical planning principles: optimum use of resources, adequate frequency, knowing the customer, research before execution, checking reach and coverage, media cost, product and business goals, campaign goals and audience profile, and negotiating with media houses. It also states that spending more money does not always ensure campaign success.

Direct and strong related coverage. These points can be organised as practical principles for examination preparation.

6. Allocation of Advertising Budget

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
The advertising management section directly discusses budget formulation. It says campaign goals and objectives should be determined first and the budget set accordingly. It lists techniques based on previous expenditure, percentage of sales, matching competition and objective-based budgeting.

Direct and very strong coverage. This is one of the best-covered Unit 4 topics in the supplied material.

7. Advertising Objectives in Campaign Planning

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
The source explains general objectives—inform, persuade and remind—and specific objectives such as inducing trial, intensifying usage, sustaining preference, confirming image, changing habits and building a line of acceptance. In strategic planning, objectives may be short-term or long-term and direct or indirect.

Direct supporting coverage. These objectives should be studied before the stages of campaign planning because the source places objective-setting at the beginning of strategy.

8. Strategy Implementation

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
After planning, the source explains implementation: active involvement of employees, assessment of budget requirements, matching budget with implementation strategy, assigning roles and responsibilities, setting deadlines and preparing a communication plan.

Direct supporting topic. Although not separately named in the Unit 4 syllabus, it completes the campaign-planning sequence in the supplied source.

9. Media Selection within Campaign Planning

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
The source describes selecting media and vehicles as media scheduling, a strategic process requiring research. It recommends checking reach, coverage, cost, product nature, business goals, campaign goals and audience profile.

Direct supporting coverage. This links Unit 4 campaign planning with the media-selection material already mapped in Unit 2.

10. Evaluation and Feedback to Future Campaigns

📄 Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
The source states that measuring and evaluating advertising effectiveness is important for the current campaign and provides direction for future campaigns. It introduces R3: Reach, Resonance and Reaction.

Direct supporting coverage. Useful as the final stage after implementation, although detailed measurement techniques belong to Unit 3.

Recommended reading order: Advertising objectives → Campaign strategy → Setting objectives → Budget formulation → Creating advertising messages → Media/vehicle selection → Strategy implementation → Campaign principles/media mix → Evaluation and feedback.

Important coverage note: The supplied advertising material gives strong direct coverage of advertising strategy, objective-setting, budget formulation, message creation, media selection, implementation and effectiveness. The exact phrase 'types of approach' is not presented as a standalone heading, and consumer behaviour is covered mainly through target-audience and customer/media-consumption concepts. These distinctions are retained rather than filling gaps from outside material.
