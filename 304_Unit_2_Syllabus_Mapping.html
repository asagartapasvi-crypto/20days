2. Media Selection

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
The source explains media scheduling as a strategic process in which media planners research before making decisions. It stresses choosing cost-effective media for the focused target audience and preparing a suitable media mix.

Direct coverage. The source is useful for understanding the basic logic of media selection and media mix.

3. Newspapers

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
Newspapers are included under print advertising. The source notes their ability to reach a large audience and their position as an old and popular advertising form. It also notes limitations such as cost, lack of niche audience choice and short shelf life.

Direct coverage. Prepare advantages and limitations separately.

4. Magazines

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
Magazines are included under print advertising. The source explains that magazine contents are designed for selected readers, allowing advertisers to choose target consumers more strategically. Magazine advertisements also have better colour reproduction and longer viewing life than newspaper advertisements.

Direct coverage. Focus on selective audience, quality of reproduction and shelf life, along with cost limitations.

5. Radio

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
Radio advertising is treated as an audio medium. Messages must be designed for sound. It is less expensive than television, but advertising duration is short and audience segmentation is not strong. Channel switching is comparatively less because people may listen while doing other activities.

Direct coverage. Prepare as a separate medium with strengths and limitations.

6. Television

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
Television is described as a popular and dominant mass medium. It reaches large audiences quickly, provides maximum exposure and combines audio and visual communication. Limitations include high cost, limited audience choice and short advertisement duration, with most ads lasting around 30–45 seconds.

Direct and strong coverage. This is also useful for the related 303 syllabus topic on TV advertisements.

7. Direct Mail

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
Direct mail is included under other media. Catalogues, brochures, sales letters, prospectuses and similar materials are used. Direct mail can reach customers personally, keeps the message under the sender's control and can reduce visibility to competitors.

Direct coverage, although it appears under “Other Media” rather than as a separate chapter.

8. Outdoor Advertising

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
Outdoor advertising includes hoardings, pole advertisements, electronic displays, wall paintings, banners, posters and transit advertising. It is useful when target customers are outside the home, can be cost-effective and offers flexibility in placement.

Direct coverage. The source also identifies limitations: mobile audiences make long messages unsuitable and audience selection is limited.

9. Hoarding

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
Hoardings are specifically listed among the outdoor advertising tools. The source places them within outdoor media used to reach people outside the home.

Direct but brief coverage. A detailed standalone treatment of hoarding specifications or formats was not identified in the supplied source.

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf

18. Media Mix – Important Supporting Concept

Sem3 304 ADVERTISING.pdf
Media mix means the right combination of media to reach target audiences. The source advises optimum use of resources, adequate frequency, understanding customer media consumption, research before campaign execution, checking reach and coverage, media cost, product and audience profile, and negotiating with media houses.

Direct and very useful supporting concept for Unit 2. This should be prepared along with media selection.
