Syllabus-to-Source Mapping

Paper: 305 – Freelance Journalism

Syllabus: Writing Process: Looking for story ideas, Planning, generating material, organizing material, selecting appropriate format, draft and edit – language elements etc. Different genres of media writing. Forms of Journalistic writing: News writing, column writing, article writing, feature writing, humour writing, editorial writing.

Mapping approach: The syllabus is treated as the controlling source. The supplied files are mapped topic-by-topic, with Direct / Strong / Related / Partial / Gap coverage clearly identified. No unsupported material is presented as if it were in the source.

1. Writing Process – Overall

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The supplied analytical-writing material provides practical guidance for journalistic writing, especially feature, opinion, editorial and magazine writing. It includes choosing topics, preparation, tapping sources, researching, planning and writing different forms.

Strong related coverage. This is the main supplied writing source for Unit 1, although a single chapter titled exactly 'Writing Process' was not identified.

2. Looking for Story Ideas

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The material advises writers to remain alert for ideas, develop original ideas, discover new subjects and maintain personal sources such as newspaper clippings, magazines, books, contacts and references for fresh assignments.

Direct related coverage. Particularly useful for the 'looking for story ideas' part of the syllabus.

3. Planning

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
Feature-writing material includes choosing a topic and preparation before writing. Opinion writing also discusses planning and writing an opinion piece.

Strong related coverage. Use the topic-selection and preparation stages as the planning component.

4. Generating Material

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The feature-writing section includes tapping sources for material and researching the topic. The material also stresses collecting background information and using personal sources for assignments.

Direct related coverage. Source-gathering and research are the main mechanisms for generating material.

5. Organizing Material

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The magazine-writing discussion stresses precision, orderliness, chronological organisation and clear presentation of researched material. It also describes a tight design in which a cogent theme is explained point by point.

Direct related coverage, especially for organisation of magazine/feature material.

6. Selecting Appropriate Format

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The material distinguishes news writing, feature writing, editorial writing, opinion writing and magazine writing, showing that different forms have different purposes, structures and styles.

Strong related coverage. The comparison of journalistic forms helps decide which format suits a particular subject.

7. Draft and Edit

📄 302 Sem3 Unit 4 Editing.pdf
The editing source explains structural editing, copy editing and rewriting. Copy editing checks and corrects written material for readability, accuracy, spelling, grammar, punctuation and formatting, while rewriting involves writing again and verifying facts, figures and formats.

Direct and strong coverage. This is the best supplied source for the draft-and-edit part of Unit 1.

8. Language Elements

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The writing material stresses simple, imaginative introductions, smooth and flowing language, precision, sincerity, transparency and simplicity. It also discusses imagery, word choice, quotations and a writing style marked by clarity, force and flavour.

Direct related coverage. Strong source for language/style in journalistic writing.

9. Different Genres of Media Writing

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The supplied block contains Feature Writing, Opinion Writing, Editorial Writing and Writing for Magazines. It also distinguishes these from news and other journalistic writings.

Strong coverage. Use this as the framework for understanding different journalistic/media-writing genres.

10. News Writing

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The material compares feature writing with news writing. News is described as objective, topical, fresh and fact-oriented, commonly using the 5Ws and 1H and an inverted-pyramid structure. News writing is concise and time-sensitive.

Strong related coverage. A separate full news-writing unit is not the main focus of this file, but the comparison provides useful exam material.

11. Column Writing

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The material discusses columns as part of newspaper editorial content and gives examples such as motoring, gardening, public grievances, comic strips, cartoons, restaurants/hotels, food and fashion. It also refers to the column writer and audience.

Related coverage. A standalone detailed column-writing chapter was not identified in the supplied material.

12. Article Writing

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The feature-writing material is directly useful for article construction: definition, ingredients, types, choosing a topic, preparation, sources, research and writing. Magazine-writing style also provides guidance on introductions, themes, paragraphs, quotations and illustrations.

Strong related coverage. Feature/article writing concepts overlap substantially, but the source does not present a standalone general 'Article Writing' unit.

13. Feature Writing

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
Unit 5 is specifically Feature Writing. It covers definition, ingredients, difference between feature and news writing, types/categories, choosing a topic, preparation, sources, research and freelancing features.

Direct and very strong match. Primary source for the feature-writing portion of Unit 1.

14. Humour Writing

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
The magazine-writing discussion mentions the occasional use of jokes, anecdotes and other engaging elements, but does not provide a dedicated treatment of humour writing as a journalistic form.

Partial/gap. Do not treat the brief references to jokes as a complete source for humour writing.

15. Editorial Writing

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
Unit 7 is Editorial Writing. It covers the newspaper editorial, history and evolution, editorial and readers, content, features, language, types, preparation, writing an editorial and the changing media scenario.

Direct and very strong match. Primary source for editorial writing.

16. Opinion Writing – Supporting Genre

📄 302 Sem3 Ubit 2 ANALYTICAL WRITING.pdf
Unit 6 – Opinion Writing covers definition, components, differences from editorials and features, major subject areas, planning and writing an opinion piece.

Direct supporting coverage. Although opinion writing is not separately named in the 305 Unit I list, it is an important neighbouring genre in the supplied source.

17. Digital / Contemporary Writing Context

📄 Sem3_BNM-021 JOURNALISM AND MEDIA IN THE DIGITAL AGE.pdf
The digital-age source discusses AI-powered writing and automated journalism, including content production, data mining, content optimisation and dissemination. It also notes the need to retain human judgement, creativity and ethical responsibility.

Supporting/contemporary context only. It can enrich answers about changing writing practices, but it is not a substitute for the core syllabus topics.

Recommended reading order: Writing process overview → story ideas → topic selection and planning → sources/research → organising material → selecting genre/format → drafting → editing/rewriting → language/style → news → column → article → feature → humour → editorial.

Important coverage note: The supplied syllabus names several forms individually. The Analytical Writing source gives especially strong treatment of feature, opinion, editorial and magazine writing, while the Editing source provides strong support for drafting, copy-editing and rewriting. Column writing and humour writing are not developed as standalone units in the identified source, so they are explicitly marked as related or partial/gap rather than being filled with unsupported content.
