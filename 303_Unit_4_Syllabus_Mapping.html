1. Origin and Growth of Radio Network

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The Radio unit traces the development of radio in India from early broadcasting experiments through state involvement, the formation and expansion of All India Radio, rural radio initiatives and the later opening of the airwaves.

Direct and strong coverage. Main source for the historical development of radio in India.

2. Radio in Colonial India

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The material describes early broadcasting, the transition to All India Radio in 1936, expansion of stations such as Lucknow and Madras, external services, rural broadcasting experiments and nationalist counter-broadcasting.

Important background for the 'origin and growth' part of the syllabus.

3. All India Radio (AIR / Akashvani)

📄 Sem3_GOVERNMENT MEDIA.pdf
The Government Media material states that on 8 June 1936 the name was changed to All India Radio. It describes post-independence expansion, the acquisition of Mysore Akashvani in 1950, the network's growth and major programme categories including music, drama, news, Vividh Bharati, sports, school broadcasts and audience research.

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The radio unit discusses AIR's historical development, public-service role, rural broadcasting, FM/AM/SW services and the later presence of private and community radio.

Very strong coverage. Use both sources: Government Media for institutional history and the HDAC Block-3 source for broader historical development.

4. Growth and Reach of AIR

📄 Sem3_GOVERNMENT MEDIA.pdf
The source records the growth from a network of 6 stations in 1947 to a much larger Akashvani network and lists its wide range of programme services.

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The source gives a later description of AIR's national reach and its operation on FM, AM and SW frequencies.

Use as supporting factual material for explaining AIR's expansion and reach. Preserve the figures as given in the source when using them in exam notes.

5. Doordarshan

📄 Sem3_GOVERNMENT MEDIA.pdf
Doordarshan began as a Delhi centre on 15 September 1959. The material describes expansion to several centres by 1975, national and colour programming from 15 August 1982, news and topical programmes, satellite directorate functions, growth in transmitters and major DD channels.

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The source discusses Doordarshan's development, expansion, programming, autonomy debate, political control and the later transition toward a more competitive broadcasting environment.

Strong coverage. Main sources for the origin, growth and institutional development of Doordarshan.

6. Prasar Bharati

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The material discusses the proposed autonomy of AIR and Doordarshan, the Akash Bharati proposal, the Prasar Bharati Bill, the 1989 proposal for autonomy and later debates over the autonomy of official electronic media.

Strong related coverage of the autonomy background and Prasar Bharati development. A complete current organisational/legal description is not provided in the identified section, so do not treat it as fully covered.

7. Organisational Structure

📄 Sem3_GOVERNMENT MEDIA.pdf
The Government Media source provides organisational context for broadcasting and describes directorate-level arrangements for Doordarshan, including a satellite directorate and centres.

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The material discusses state-controlled AIR and Doordarshan, institutional control, autonomy proposals and the changing relationship between government and broadcasting.

Related/partial coverage. The sources support institutional structure and control, but do not provide a complete current organisational chart of AIR, Doordarshan or Prasar Bharati.

8. FM Radio

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The source describes the opening of airwaves to private players after the 1995 Supreme Court ruling, the growth of private FM radio, the urban-centred character of private FM and the development of community radio. It also notes that AIR operates on FM, AM and SW.

📄 Sem3_GOVERNMENT MEDIA.pdf
The source provides the institutional broadcasting background of AIR and its programme services.

Direct and strong coverage for the development of private FM and its place alongside public and community radio.

9. Private FM and Community Radio

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The source explains private FM growth, licensing, urban orientation, restrictions on news, community-radio advocacy, the 2006 community radio policy and subsequent growth in community radio stations.

Strong supporting topic for FM radio. Useful for a short note comparing public AIR, private FM and community radio.

10. Radio after the Opening of Airwaves

📄 301 Sem3 Devlp Jurnl_HDAC Block-3.pdf
The source describes the Supreme Court's 1995 airwaves ruling, the entry of private players, growth of FM radio, community radio policy and the changing role of the state from broadcaster/controller toward an enabler.

Important for explaining the transition from a predominantly state-controlled radio environment to a mixed broadcasting system.
